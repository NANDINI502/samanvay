## Hepatitis B — transmission

- In highly endemic areas, most commonly spread mother-to-child at birth or
  child-to-child in the first 5 years of life.
- Other routes: needlestick injuries, tattooing, piercing, contaminated
  injection equipment, sexual contact.
- Infection in adulthood becomes chronic in less than 5% of cases; in infancy
  or early childhood, about 95%.
(WHO)

## Hepatitis B — symptoms and complications

- Most newly infected people have no symptoms.
- Some have illness lasting several weeks: jaundice, dark urine, fatigue,
  nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain.
- Severe acute hepatitis can lead to liver failure.
- Chronic infection can progress to cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma.
(WHO)

## Hepatitis B — testing

- Hepatitis B cannot be told apart from other viral hepatitis clinically;
  lab confirmation (HBsAg) is essential.
- Where HBsAg seroprevalence is >2%, all adults should be offered testing.
- In all settings, test people with suspected liver disease, blood donors,
  and all pregnant women.
- Priority groups include partners and family of infected people, health-care
  workers, people who inject drugs, and people living with HIV.
(WHO)

## Hepatitis B — prevention and treatment

- First vaccine dose as soon as possible after birth (within 24 hours), then
  two or three doses at least four weeks apart; boosters usually not needed.
- No specific treatment for acute hepatitis B — supportive care.
- Chronic infection is treated with oral medicines such as tenofovir or
  entecavir; most people who start must continue for life.
(WHO)

## Cirrhosis — symptoms

- Early: feeling tired or weak, itching of the skin, poor appetite; may be no
  symptoms at first.
- Complications: jaundice; swelling of the abdomen from fluid (ascites);
  confusion, difficulty thinking, memory loss, personality changes or sleep
  disorders; internal bleeding from burst enlarged veins (varices) in the
  esophagus, stomach, or intestines.
- Common causes: heavy alcohol use, fatty liver disease, chronic hepatitis B
  or C.
(NIDDK)

## Fatty liver disease (NAFLD / MASLD)

- Fat builds up in the liver; the inflammatory form (NASH / MASH) involves
  inflammation and liver damage.
- More likely with obesity, metabolic syndrome, and type 2 diabetes.
- Usually silent, with few or no symptoms.
- Diagnosed using history, exam, blood tests, imaging, and sometimes biopsy.
- Weight loss can reduce liver fat, inflammation, and fibrosis.
(NIDDK)
