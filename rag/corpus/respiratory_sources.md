## COPD — what it is and symptoms

- A common lung disease causing restricted airflow and breathing problems;
  also called emphysema or chronic bronchitis.
- Common symptoms: breathlessness, chronic cough (sometimes with phlegm),
  fatigue; usually appearing from mid-life and worsening over time.
- Raises risk of lung infections, lung cancer, heart problems, weak muscles,
  brittle bones, depression and anxiety.
(WHO)

## COPD — risk factors

- Tobacco smoke (active or second-hand): over 70% of cases in high-income
  countries; 30–40% in low- and middle-income countries.
- Household air pollution from biomass fuels and coal for cooking/heating is
  a major risk factor in low- and middle-income countries.
- Also: occupational dust, fumes and chemicals; poor growth before birth;
  premature birth; severe childhood respiratory infections; childhood asthma;
  alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency.
(WHO)

## COPD — diagnosis

- Suspect COPD in people with typical symptoms.
- Diagnosis is confirmed with spirometry, a breathing test of lung function.
- Spirometry is often unavailable in low- and middle-income countries, so
  COPD can be missed there.
(WHO)

## COPD — management and flare-ups

- Cannot be cured, but improves with not smoking, avoiding air pollution,
  and vaccines (flu, pneumonia, COVID-19).
- Bronchodilator inhalers are the most important medicines: short-acting
  (work within seconds, last about 4–6 hours) and long-acting (daily).
- Inhaled corticosteroids, sometimes with bronchodilators; oxygen for severe
  disease; pulmonary rehabilitation; correct inhaler technique with a spacer.
- Flare-ups: symptoms worsen quickly, last a few days, often triggered by a
  respiratory infection; may need antibiotics, steroid tablets, or extra
  inhaled/nebulised medicine.
(WHO)

## Asthma — symptoms and triggers

- Persistent cough (especially at night), wheezing, shortness of breath,
  chest tightness; often worse at night or with exercise.
- Triggers: colds, weather changes, dust, smoke, pollen, animal fur, strong
  fragrances.
- Risk factors: close relative with asthma, eczema or hay fever, low birth
  weight, prematurity, tobacco smoke, air pollution, viral infections, house
  dust mites, mould, workplace chemicals, overweight or obesity.
(WHO)

## Asthma — treatment

- Bronchodilators (e.g. salbutamol) open the air passages and relieve
  symptoms.
- Inhaled steroids (e.g. beclometasone) reduce airway inflammation, improving
  symptoms and lowering the risk of severe attacks and death.
- Combination inhalers contain both; daily use may be needed.
- A spacer helps; a homemade spacer from a 500 ml plastic bottle can work as
  well as a commercial one.
- Severe attacks may need emergency care or hospital admission and can be
  fatal; an asthma action plan explains how to step up treatment.
(WHO)

## Limits of this corpus
