## Stroke warning signs — F.A.S.T.

- Face: ask the person to smile. Does one side of the face droop?
- Arms: ask the person to raise both arms. Does one arm drift downward?
- Speech: ask the person to repeat a simple phrase. Is the speech slurred or
  strange?
- Time: if any of these three signs appear, call emergency services right
  away.
(NINDS)

## Other sudden stroke symptoms

Call emergency services immediately if one or more appear suddenly:
- Trouble seeing in one or both eyes
- Dizziness, or loss of balance or coordination
- Severe headache with no known cause
(NINDS)

## What to do immediately

- Call emergency services as soon as any sign is seen.
- The person should not drive themselves or be driven to hospital — an
  ambulance lets staff begin treatment on the way.
- Note the time when symptoms first started.
(NINDS)

## Why time matters

- The best stroke treatments can only be used within 4.5 hours of the first
  symptoms.
- Some people benefit from treatment up to 24 hours after a stroke, so a
  suspected stroke still needs hospital care promptly, even if symptoms began
  during sleep.
(NINDS)

## Referral (WHO PEN, Action 4)

- Known stroke or transient ischemic attack (TIA) → refer to the next level
  of care.

## Limits of this corpus

Covers stroke recognition only. No epilepsy, dementia, Parkinson's disease,
migraine management, or neuro-imaging interpretation. Any acute neurological
symptom is an emergency referral, not something this agent manages.
