## Types of diabetes

- Type 1: deficient insulin production; requires daily insulin. Cause and
  prevention not known.
- Type 2: more than 95% of people with diabetes; often preventable.
- Gestational: raised blood glucose during pregnancy, found through prenatal
  screening.
- Impaired glucose tolerance (IGT) and impaired fasting glycaemia (IFG) carry
  a high risk of progressing to type 2.
(WHO)

## Symptoms

- Feeling very thirsty, urinating more often, blurred vision, tiredness,
  unintentional weight loss.
- Type 2 symptoms can be mild and go unnoticed for years.
(WHO)

## Diagnostic thresholds (WHO PEN)

- Fasting blood sugar ≥7 mmol/L (126 mg/dL), or
- Random blood sugar ≥11.1 mmol/L (200 mg/dL).

## Complications and screening

- Heart and blood vessels: higher risk of heart attack and stroke.
- Kidney: higher risk of kidney failure — screen for kidney disease.
- Eye: can cause permanent vision loss — eye exams for retinopathy.
- Nerves and feet: nerve damage and poor blood flow can cause foot ulcers and
  amputation — foot care.
(WHO)

## Prevention and management

- Lifestyle is the best way to prevent or delay type 2: healthy weight, at
  least 150 minutes of moderate exercise per week, a diet limiting sugar and
  saturated fat, no tobacco.
- Medicines for blood sugar include insulin, metformin, sulfonylureas and
  SGLT-2 inhibitors; insulin is required in type 1.
- People with diabetes often also need blood pressure medication and statins.
(WHO)
- BP threshold for antihypertensive drugs in diabetes: persistent ≥130/80
  mmHg (WHO PEN).

## Limits of this corpus

No HbA1c targets, no insulin dosing, no diabetic ketoacidosis or
hypoglycaemia management, no thyroid/adrenal/other endocrine conditions.
Confusion, vomiting with high sugar, or very low sugar are emergencies to
refer, not manage.
