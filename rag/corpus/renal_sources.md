## Who should be tested for kidney disease

- Get checked for kidney disease if the person has diabetes, high blood
  pressure, heart disease, or a family history of kidney failure (NIDDK).
- People with diabetes: get checked every year (NIDDK).
- High blood pressure, heart disease, or family history: ask the provider how
  often to test (NIDDK).
- "Testing is the only way to know how well your kidneys are working." (NIDDK)

## Blood test: GFR (glomerular filtration rate)

- GFR of 60 or more is in the normal range.
- GFR of less than 60 may mean kidney disease.
- GFR of 15 or less is called kidney failure.
(NIDDK)

## Urine test: albumin (UACR)

- Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio of 30 mg/g or less is normal.
- More than 30 mg/g may be a sign of kidney disease.
- An abnormal albumin result may be repeated one or two more times to
  confirm it.
- A dipstick test changes color when albumin is present.
(NIDDK)

## Blood pressure in kidney disease (WHO PEN)

- Persistent BP ≥130/80 mmHg → consider antihypertensive drugs in patients
  with diabetes or albuminuria (a stricter threshold than ≥140/90 for the
  general population).

## Referral criteria relevant to the kidney (WHO PEN, Action 4)

- Known kidney disease → refer to the next level of care.
- Any proteinuria → refer.
- BP >200/>120 mmHg → urgent referral.

## Limits of this corpus

No formal CKD staging (G1–G5 / A1–A3), no dialysis or transplant guidance,
no drug-dosing-by-GFR rules, no acute kidney injury criteria. Queries needing
these should be deferred to a nephrologist.
